2. Grammar: Giving Advice & Imagining

We use the Second Conditional not only for dreams, but to give strong advice or to imagine ourselves in someone else's position.

Giving Advice
If I were you, I would + Verb

This is the best way to say "Yo en tu lugar haría..."

"If I were you, I would study more."
Role Reversal
If I were the [Title], I would...

Use this to explain what you would change if you had the power.

"If I were the Headmaster, I would change the rules."
🚨 THE GRAMMAR REBEL: "WERE"

Normally, with the pronoun "I", we use "was" (I was happy). But in conditionals, grammar rebels! To show that the situation is 100% imaginary, we always use WERE for all subjects (I, He, She, It).

❌ If I was the Headmaster... (Common mistake)
✅ If I WERE the Headmaster... (Perfect English)

Reading Context: Teacher Ivan's Rules

"I love teaching English, Philosophy, and History at Colegio Fielding. But sometimes I wonder: What would happen if I were the Headmaster? Well, I would make some big changes!"

"First, I would change the Physical Education timetable. If I were in charge, I would include CrossFit in the morning! Second, I currently teach History of Bolivia only to 1st, 2nd, 3rd, and 4th grade. If I were the Headmaster, I would make History mandatory for 5th and 6th grades too."

"However, there is one thing I wouldn't change: the evaluation system. The weights are perfect: 45 points for Saber, 40 points for Hacer, 10 points for Ser, and 5 points for self-evaluation. If another school asked for my advice, I would say: 'If I were you, I would use this exact grading system.' Oh, and finally, Perlita the cat would be the official school mascot!"
