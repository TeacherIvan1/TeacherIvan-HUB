2. Grammar: Future with "Will"

We use "Will" for the future, but NOT for organized plans (for plans, we use 'going to'). We use "Will" for promises, predictions, and sudden decisions we make in the moment.

Affirmative (+)
Subject + Will + Verb
"I will code tonight." (Promise)
"It will be a great app." (Prediction)
"I will help you." (Sudden decision)
Negative (-)
Subject + Won't + Verb

*Note: "Won't" is the short form of "Will not".

"I won't stop trying."
"The website won't fail."
🚨 THE "WILL TO" TRAP

Will is a modal verb. You must never use the word "to" after it. Also, the action verb never changes (no "s", no "ing", no "ed").

❌ I will to build a website.
❌ He will learns English.
✅ I WILL BUILD a website. / He WILL LEARN English.

Reading Context: The Origin of the HUB

"One day at Colegio Fielding, I noticed my students needed a modern platform to practice English. It was a big problem. Suddenly, I had an idea: I will build a website for them! It will be called the 'Teacher Ivan HUB'."

"I made a promise to my students: 'I will code every night until it is perfect. I won't let you down!' Lau supported my idea. She said: 'It will be an amazing tool for education in Sucre.'"

"Meanwhile, I looked at El Michi next to my computer. Will he help me? No, he won't write any HTML. I am sure he will step on my keyboard and sleep! But that's okay, I will do the hard work."
