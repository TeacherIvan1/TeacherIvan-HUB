2. Grammar: As ... As

We already know how to say one thing is "bigger" or "faster". But how do we say two things are equal? Or how do we say they are NOT equal without using "-er"? We use the structure as [adjective] as.

Equality (+)
as + ADJECTIVE + as

It means "Tan... como". They are exactly the same.

"Lau is as fast as Ivan."
"This barbell is as heavy as that one."
Inequality (-)
NOT as + ADJECTIVE + as

It means "No es tan... como". The first object is LESS than the second.

"Sucre is not as cold as La Paz."
"Running is not as difficult as CrossFit."
🚨 THE DOUBLE COMPARATIVE TRAP

When you use "as ... as", the adjective inside MUST be in its normal, base form. You cannot add "-er" or "more". Save those for when you use "than"!

❌ La Paz is as colder as Oruro.
❌ Lau is not as faster as me.
✅ La Paz is as COLD as Oruro. / Lau is not as FAST as me.

Reading Context: City Contrasts

"As a History teacher at Colegio Fielding, I love teaching my students about the beautiful cities of Bolivia. I live in Sucre, the capital. Sucre is a beautiful city, but it is not as big as La Paz. Also, Sucre is very peaceful, while La Paz is as crowded as a huge metropolis!"

"Sometimes, Lau and I travel to other cities for CrossFit competitions. Training in La Paz is a big challenge. Sucre is not as high as La Paz, so breathing there is difficult! Besides, the weather is totally different. The mornings in Sucre are not as cold as the mornings in La Paz. Doing a Clean & Jerk at 5:30 AM in La Paz is freezing!"

"What about El Michi? Does he want to travel? Absolutely not. He prefers our home. For him, sleeping on our sofa in Sucre is as comfortable as sleeping in a 5-star hotel!"
