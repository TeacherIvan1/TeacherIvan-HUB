Objective: Learn to describe interrupted actions using the Past Continuous and Past Simple.

1. Vocabulary: Sudden Interruptions

Learn these verbs to describe short, sudden actions that interrupt your routine.

🌧️ Start to rain Empezar a llover Past: Started
📱 Ring Sonar (el teléfono) Past: Rang
🦶 Trip and fall Tropezar y caer Past: Tripped and fell
💥 Drop Dejar caer Past: Dropped

2. Grammar: When vs. While

To tell a good story in English, we often mix two past tenses. We use the Past Continuous for a long action in progress, and the Past Simple for a short, sudden action that interrupts it.

The Long Action (While)
While + Subject + was/were + V-ing

While means "Mientras". Use it for actions that take time.

"While I was running in Sucre..."
"While she was sleeping..."
The Short Action (When)
When + Subject + Past Simple Verb

When means "Cuando". Use it for the sudden interruption.

"...when the phone rang."
"...when it started to rain."
🚨 THE FATAL MIX-UP

Never use "While" for a short, sudden action. You cannot say "mientras el teléfono sonó" in English.

❌ I was taking a shower while the phone rang.
✅ I was taking a shower WHEN the phone rang.

Reading Context: A Crazy Thursday Morning

"Yesterday was Thursday. While I was running at 5:30 AM in Sucre, the weather changed. I was feeling great, but suddenly, it started to rain very hard! I ran back home immediately."

"Later, while I was taking a shower, my phone rang. I ran out of the shower wet. It was a student from Colegio Fielding asking about the Philosophy homework!"

"And it gets worse. While I was talking on the phone with the student, Perlita jumped on the table. When she jumped, she dropped my glass of water on my HTML coding notes. What a crazy morning!"
