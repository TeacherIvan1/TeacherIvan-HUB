When the cat got sick

Objective: Link past events using SO (result) and BECAUSE (reason) in a narrative.

1. Vocabulary: Health Emergencies

Learn these verbs to describe unexpected health issues in a story.

🤮 Throw up Vomitar Past: Threw up
🚑 Rush to Ir corriendo a (con prisa) Past: Rushed to
🤒 Get sick Enfermarse Past: Got sick
❤️‍🩹 Recover Recuperarse Past: Recovered

2. Grammar: Because vs. So

To make a story interesting, you cannot just list disconnected facts. You must link actions using SO (for results) and BECAUSE (for reasons).

BECAUSE (Reason)
Action + BECAUSE + Reason

Answers the question: Why did this happen?

"We rushed to the vet because we were worried."
SO (Result)
Reason + SO + Action

Explains the consequence: Therefore / As a result.

"We were worried, so we rushed to the vet."
🚨 THE DOUBLE CONNECTOR TRAP

In Spanish, it is common to mix reasons and results heavily, but in English, NEVER use "Because" and "So" in the same sentence to describe the same event. Choose one!

❌ Because she was sick, so we went to the doctor.
✅ She was sick, SO we went to the doctor.
✅ We went to the doctor BECAUSE she was sick.

Reading Context: The Veterinary Emergency

"Last month, my girlfriend and I had a big scare. While we were packing our gym bags for a CrossFit competition in Sucre, we noticed Perlita. She was sleeping on the sofa, as usual, but suddenly she made a strange noise."

"While we were watching her, she threw up on the floor! We were very worried about her, so we cancelled our training session immediately. We rushed to the vet because she looked very weak."

"The vet checked her while she was shaking. Fortunately, it wasn't serious. She got sick because she ate too much tuna! The vet gave her some medicine, so she recovered quickly. Now, we are very careful with her diet!"
