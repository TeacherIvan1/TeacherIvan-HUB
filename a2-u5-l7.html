Don't have to vs Mustn't

Objective: Distinguish between things that are optional and things that are strictly prohibited.

1. Vocabulary: Rules and Choices

Learn these words to understand the severity of the instructions you receive.

🤷‍♂️ Optional Opcional
⛔ Prohibited Prohibido
⚖️ Choose Elegir
❌ Break a rule Romper una regla

2. Grammar: The Final Showdown

This is one of the most important lessons in English. Don't have to means you have a choice. Mustn't means you have NO choice; it is forbidden.

Don't / Doesn't Have To
It is NOT necessary

You can do it if you want, but it's optional.

"You don't have to wear a tie to the party." (You can, but it's okay if you don't).
"He doesn't have to work on Sunday."
Mustn't
It is STRICTLY PROHIBITED

Do NOT do it. It is dangerous or against the law.

"You mustn't park your car here." (The police will take it).
"She mustn't touch the fire."
🚨 THE DANGEROUS TRANSLATION TRAP

If you see a sign that says "No fumar", do not say "You don't have to smoke". That sounds like a friendly suggestion! If it is a rule or a law, you must use Mustn't.

❌ You don't have to cheat on the exam. (¡Suena a que copiar es opcional!)
✅ You MUSTN'T cheat on the exam. (¡Está totalmente prohibido!)

Reading Context: Options vs Rules

"In my classroom at Colegio Fielding, the instructions are clear. During an exam, you don't have to finish in 10 minutes; you can take your time. However, you mustn't use your phone. If you break the rule, you fail."

"It is the same at the CrossFit box in Sucre. Lau and I know the safety rules. You don't have to lift 140 kg today if you are tired. It is optional. But you mustn't drop the barbell directly on the floor. It is prohibited because it ruins the equipment."

"And what about El Michi? Well, he doesn't have to do anything. He has zero obligations. But there is one strict rule in the house: He mustn't scratch the sofa! (Does he listen? Of course not!)."
