Comparing Historic Eras

Objective: Compare life in the past using "was/were" and comparative adjectives.

1. Vocabulary: Time and History

🛡️ Safe Seguro

2. Grammar: Comparatives in the Past

When we compare empires, people, or situations from history, we CANNOT use "is" or "are". We must combine our comparative adjectives with the past forms of the verb To Be: WAS (singular) and WERE (plural).

Singular Past (Was)
Subject + WAS + Comparative + than

Use for one person, one empire, or uncountable nouns (like "life").

"Life was harder than today."
"The Roman Empire was bigger than Greece."
Plural Past (Were)
Subject + WERE + Comparative + than

Use for groups of people or multiple things.

"The Spartans were stronger than others."
"Ancient weapons were more dangerous."
🚨 THE PRESENT TENSE TRAP

Because history books describe facts, students often use "is" by accident (e.g., "The Roman Empire is bigger"). Remember: if the empire or the people do not exist today, you MUST use the past tense!

❌ Life in the past is harder.
❌ The Romans was more powerful. (¡Romans es plural!)
✅ Life in the past WAS harder. / The Romans WERE more powerful.

Reading Context: A History Lesson

"In my History classes at Colegio Fielding, my students love to compare the past with the present. For example, Leonardo always asks: 'Teacher, was life in Ancient Rome more dangerous than today?' Yes, it was! Ancient Rome was more powerful than many empires, but the streets were much more dangerous than modern Sucre."

"Lau and I do CrossFit, which is very difficult. But being a gladiator in the Roman Colosseum was definitely harder than a CrossFit workout! We are lucky that sports today are safer than in the past."

"And what about animals? Ancient cats (like tigers and lions from the past) were faster and more active than house cats today. When I look at El Michi sleeping on the sofa, I know the truth: he is the laziest cat in history!"
