The 5:30 AM Routine

Objective: Talk about daily schedules using Prepositions of Time (In, On, At).

1. Vocabulary: Daily Actions

Learn these essential verbs to describe the start of your day.

☀️ Get up Levantarse (de la cama)
🚿 Take a shower Tomar una ducha
☕ Have breakfast Desayunar
🚪 Leave Salir / Marcharse (de casa)

2. Grammar: In, On, At

To connect actions with the time they happen, we use three prepositions. Each one has a specific rule.

IN (Periods)
Months, Years, Parts of the day

Exceptions: We say "in the morning", "in the afternoon", "in the evening".

"I run in the morning."
ON (Days)
Days of the week & Dates

Always use "On" for any word that ends in "day".

"I teach on Monday."
AT (Exact Time)
Clock times & "Night"

Use "At" for the specific hour on the clock, and for the word "night".

"I get up at 5:30 AM."
"I sleep at night."
🚨 THE "EN" TRAP

In Spanish, we use the word "en" for almost everything ("en la mañana", "en lunes"). If you translate this directly to "in Monday", it is a serious mistake! Remember the pyramid: IN (General) ➔ ON (Days) ➔ AT (Exact time).

❌ I work in Tuesday in 8:00 AM.
✅ I work ON Tuesday AT 8:00 AM.

Reading Context: The Educator's Schedule

"My day always starts very early. I get up at 5:30 AM to go running. In the morning, the weather in Sucre is perfect for exercise. After my run, I take a shower and have breakfast. Sometimes, I enjoy a traditional salteña!"

"On weekdays, I leave home early to teach at Colegio Fielding. For example, on Mondays and Wednesdays, I teach History of Bolivia to the 1st, 2nd, 3rd, and 4th grades. I never teach History to the 5th or 6th grade!"

"My partner Lau also has a busy routine. But El Michi? He sleeps at night, and he also sleeps in the afternoon. He doesn't need to check the clock at 5:30 AM. He has absolutely no schedule!"
