2. Grammar: The "S" Rule (He / She / It)

When we talk about ONE other person or animal (He, She, It), the Present Simple verb changes! We must add an "S" (or "es" / "ies") at the end of the verb.

Affirmative (+)
He/She/It + Verb + s

Add 's' to the verb. (Study ➔ Studies).

"Maria reads a book."
"He studies English."
Negative (-)
He/She/It + doesn't + Verb

Use doesn't (does not). The verb goes back to NORMAL (no 's').

"Perlita doesn't read."
"He doesn't like math."
🚨 The Double "S" Trap

In negative sentences, the word "doesn't" already has the "S" rule inside it. Because of this, the main verb loses the "S". Never put two "S"s together!

❌ She doesn't reads books.
✅ She doesn't READ books.

Reading Context: Maria and Perlita

"Maria is a student at Colegio Fielding in Sucre. Every day, she goes to school. She studies English and Philosophy with Teacher Ivan. In class, she listens to the teacher and she writes in her notebook. She likes school!"

"But what about Perlita? Perlita is a cat. She doesn't go to school. She doesn't study English or Philosophy. Perlita doesn't read books."

"So, what does Perlita do? She sleeps on the sofa all day and she eats tuna. It is a very easy life!"
