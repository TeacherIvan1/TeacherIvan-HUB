Training Safely

Objective: Give advice and state rules using Should and Must.

1. Vocabulary: Gym Safety

Learn these words to talk about preventing accidents when you exercise.

🔥 Warm up Calentar (antes de entrenar)
🧊 Cool down Enfriar / Relajar (después)
💥 Drop Dejar caer / Soltar
🤕 Injury Lesión

2. Grammar: Should vs. Must

When you want to tell someone what to do, you have to decide if it is just a good idea (Advice) or a strict rule (Obligation).

Should / Shouldn't (Advice)
Subject + should + Verb

It means: "It is a good/bad idea." (Deberías / No deberías).

"You should warm up before you run."
"He shouldn't lift that heavy barbell."
Must / Mustn't (Rule)
Subject + must + Verb

It means: "It is an obligation or a strict rule." (Debes / Está prohibido).

"You must wear sports shoes here."
"We mustn't drop the empty bar!"
🚨 THE "TO" TRAP

Modal verbs (like should, must, can) are very special in English. You NEVER use the word "to" after them. You put the action verb directly!

❌ You must to rest.
❌ She should to drink water.
✅ You must rest. / She should drink water.

Reading Context: Rules at the Box

"CrossFit is amazing, but it can be dangerous if you don't follow the rules. In our box in Sucre, Lau and I always listen to the coach. For example, before a heavy Snatch or a Clean & Jerk, you should warm up your shoulders. If you don't, you might get an injury."

"There are also strict rules. You must use chalk if your hands are sweating, but you mustn't leave a mess on the floor! Also, the golden rule in weightlifting: You mustn't drop an empty barbell. It damages the equipment."

"After the workout, you should cool down and stretch. When I go home, I must correct exams for my students at Colegio Fielding. El Michi looks at me from the sofa. He should exercise, but he prefers his lazy life!"
