Going to vs Will

Objective: Choose correctly between organized plans and spontaneous decisions.

1. Vocabulary: Making Choices

Learn these verbs to understand the context of your future actions.

📅 Plan Planear
🤔 Decide Decidir
🤷‍♂️ Guess Adivinar / Suponer
🤝 Promise Prometer

2. Grammar: The Final Showdown

We have learned two ways to talk about the future. Now you must choose the correct one depending on WHEN you made the decision.

Be Going To (Organized Plans)

You decided this before speaking. You have tickets, dates, or a clear intention.

"We are going to travel tomorrow. We have the tickets."
"I am going to study HTML tonight."
Will (Sudden Decisions)

You decided this at the exact moment of speaking. Used for reactions, offers, and promises.

"The phone is ringing! I will answer it."
"You look tired. I will help you."
🚨 THE ABUSE OF "WILL" TRAP

Because "Will" is easier to write and pronounce than "am going to", students use it for everything. If you already planned a vacation, you CANNOT use "Will".

❌ I bought the tickets. I will fly to Santa Cruz tomorrow.
✅ I bought the tickets. I AM GOING TO FLY to Santa Cruz tomorrow.

Reading Context: A Chaotic Weekend

"This weekend is perfectly organized. Lau and I are going to travel to Santa Cruz for a big CrossFit competition. We already booked the hotel and packed our suitcases. We are going to win!"

"Suddenly, my phone rings. It's a student from Colegio Fielding. 'Teacher Ivan, the HUB is broken! The HTML code has a bug!'. It's an emergency. I look at Lau and say: 'Don't worry, I will fix it right now! I will check the computer before we leave.'"

"While I fix the code, El Michi looks at my open suitcase on the bed. I know exactly what he is thinking. I guess he will sleep on my clean clothes... Oh no, wait! He is going to jump inside right now! Someone stop him!"
